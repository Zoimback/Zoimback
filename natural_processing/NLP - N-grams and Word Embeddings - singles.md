Curious fact about probabilities
- Most text generated by machines align pretty well with the inferred

probabilities of SMT models, whereas texts written by humans tend to
give lower probabilities

- Also humans tend to have each their own style of writing and composing

sentences, so patterns can be observed here too

- Although more complex nowadays, most plagiarism detection models are

based on and work thanks to this property (among others)

Word Embeddings
- As we seen, computers like numbers/vectors, not words
- What if we want to translate words to vectors?
- First approach, assign each word an one-hot vector

V = {hello, goodbye, world}
hello -> [1 0 0]
goodbye -> [0 1 0]
world -> [0 0 1]

- Equivalently, assign each word a random number e.g.
               hello -> 1 / 3, goodbye -> 2 / 3, world -> 3 / 3

Natural Language Processing

Word Embeddings
- These two representations give no information about the very nature of

the words

- For instance, if encodings are chosen alphabetically “dog” and “diet” will

be closer than “dog” and “mammal”

Word Embeddings and Skip-Grams
- Train a simple NN to predict what word goes between two words

f(how, you) = are
f(can, go) = I
f(buy, computer) = a
…

- Construct the NN so its first layer will be just a linear transformation
- Non-linear transformations are also possible but they also increase the
amount of required data to avoid overfitting (and make things more
expensive!)

- Some word embeddings are trained predicting the next word based on an

n-gram prefix, and others are way more complex
